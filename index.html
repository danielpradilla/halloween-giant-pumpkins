The arms race

How big can they get?

These are the main reasons for the growth. No source predicts where the record will stop.

  • Breeding. Howard Dill, a grower in Nova Scotia, spent about 30 years breeding the Atlantic Giant. Every world champion since then comes from his seeds (Oregon State University).
  • Genetics. Giant varieties do not make more sugar than other pumpkins. They move more sugar into the fruit (Savage et al., 2015).
  • Growing. Growers keep one pumpkin per plant, give each plant about 1,000 square feet (93 m²), pollinate by hand and shade the fruit (Ohio State University).
  • Water. One plant can use up to 500 US gallons (1,900 liters) of water a week. At its fastest, a pumpkin gains close to 50 lb (23 kg) a day (Oregon State University, Smithsonian).
  • Limits. Big pumpkins sag under their own weight and can be up to 50% flatter than small ones. They crack when they grow too fast, for example after heavy rain (Hu et al., 2011).
